## SCREEN 01 — THE FAMILIAR IDEA BEHIND LEVERAGE

# Put More of the Growth Opportunity to Work.

A mortgage helps someone own a larger Home than their cash alone could buy. An approved GPA credit line uses a similar idea to hold a larger investment.

## SCREEN 02 — THE IDEA IN NUMBERS

Your money: **$200,000** + approved borrowing: **$200,000** in GPA shares = **$400,000**.

**2,000** shares with cash alone. **4,000** starting shares with the LOC.

When share-value growth exceeds interest and other costs, holding more shares can increase what remains yours. Gia helps you understand the plan.

Month-1 entry at $100 per share. More shares can magnify gains and losses. Credit requires approval and is not available to everyone.

## SCREEN 03 — UNDERSTANDING YOUR LOC

# A Credit Line Can Help Your Money Do More.

Continue holding eligible shares while using approved borrowing for a defined purpose.

Example: $400,000 in eligible shares. $200,000 total debt limit; $200,000 equity at the limit.

**Buy more GPA.** Increase participation in investment growth.

**Keep cash available.** Retain shares while using approved credit.

**Fund Home Fees.** Finance housing and interest rather than pay from cash.

Example APR: Year 1 12% / Year 2 10% / Year 3 8%.

Draws remain repayable. Interest paid with draws increases debt. Falling accepted values may require cash paydown.

**Choice 3 — Live in your Home. Keep monthly cash available.** Monthly out-of-pocket cash: $0 for all 36 modeled months. The LOC pays the $7,500 Home Fee and its interest. 4,000 shares. **Choice 4 — Fund Home life. Put more shares to work.** Monthly out-of-pocket cash: $0. The LOC pays Home Fees and interest, then buys more GPA with remaining capacity. Year-3 share value $3,252,359. LOC debt $1,626,179. Net equity $1,626,179 (+$1,426,179).

**Without Glonari.** Ownership budget $8,155.75 a month, including $5,322.42 mortgage payment plus assumed tax, insurance, upkeep and HOA. Net home equity at year 3: $383,809 (home value $1,157,625; mortgage balance $773,816). Three-year member cash with Glonari: $470,000 including entry.

## SCREEN 07 — UNDERSTANDING THE EXAMPLES

# A Few Things to Know Before You Choose.

**What does $0 mean?** After the initial $200,000, Choices 3 and 4 fund the modeled $7,500 Home Fee and LOC interest through borrowing for 36 months. Housing is financed, not free. Other living costs, separate charges or collateral shortfalls may need cash.

**How is the LOC modeled?** Debt stays within 50% of lender-accepted share value. APR: 12%, 10%, 8% in years 1, 2, 3, charged monthly on opening debt. New shares immediately qualify as collateral. Choice 4 reinvests remaining room and ends at the limit.

**What supports GPA value?** The model combines properties acquired without property mortgages and an operating business intended to earn recurring revenue. Estimated year-end share values: $252.22, $325.41 and $402.32 from a $100 initial entry.

**Can values fall?** Yes. The company model assumes 5% annual property appreciation, monthly acquisitions and immediate operating value for fully active Homes. Business value depends on executed operations and contracts. Properties and business performance can weaken.

**What are the mortgage terms in the comparison?** $1 million Home; $200,000 down; $800,000 loan; 30 years, 7% fixed; $5,322.42 monthly principal and interest. Home growth: 5% annually, compounded monthly.

**What makes the $8,155.75 budget?** Add monthly tax $1,250, insurance $500, upkeep reserve $833.33 and HOA $250. Proposed estimates, held flat, not verified quotes. Compare equivalent Glonari coverage and add uncovered costs. Upkeep is a reserve, not necessarily spent.

Examples are not guarantees or cash sale proceeds. Taxes, lender fees, collateral discounts, credit reductions and sale costs are not modeled. Falling collateral can require cash or a forced sale. GPA resale needs a permitted transaction and buyer. Credit is not assured. Mortgage equity includes principal repayment and assumed 5% property appreciation. Greater returns or lower overall risk are not assured. GPA and Home equity are different assets. A traditional budget assumes equivalent coverage; verify bills. Actual credit availability, lender terms and permitted uses must be confirmed.
